Academic Methodology & Audits

Frequently Examined Questions

Detailed technical explanations answering questions regarding temporal leakage, spatial cross-validation, and data artifacts.

Why did you reject random k-fold cross-validation in favor of District GroupKFold?
Adjacent hoblis within the same taluk or district share identical synoptic weather patterns. A standard random k-fold cross-validation allows contiguous stations to leak into validation folds, producing artificially inflated evaluation scores. We implemented 5-Fold District-Grouped Cross-Validation (GroupKFold across 31 districts), guaranteeing that an entire district is evaluated strictly out-of-fold.
What causes the Agumbe data-quality artifact?
In 2024, Agumbe received an astounding 5,383.1 mm of rainfall, yet was classified as "drought-prone" with a departure of -28.84%. This occurs because KSNDMC records assign Agumbe a fixed static normal of 7,565.0 mm. This is not meteorological aridity; it is a data-quality artifact resulting from an uncalibrated historical benchmark. We flagged 338 such stations in reports/suspect_normals.csv.
Why was Ridge Regression permanently dropped from deployment?
Ridge regression produced a catastrophic test MAE of 53.79%. Because our training seasons experienced a continuous wet cycle (2020: +21%, 2021: +28%, 2022: +51%), the monotonically increasing n_prior_years index caused Ridge to learn a false linear time trend (+20.15 coefficient). In 2025 where n_prior_years = 4, it extrapolated positive anomalies to over +80%, resulting in gross errors.
How does the pipeline handle the missing year 2023?
KSNDMC published no hobli-level annual tables for calendar year 2023. Rather than silently imputing synthetic figures, we explicitly parameterized the jump via gap_years = 2 between 2022 and 2024. This prevents tree algorithms from treating the lag features as immediate single-year shifts.
